Perlite is volcanic glass — specifically a form of hydrated obsidian — that has been superheated to 1,600°F (871°C) until it expands like popcorn to 13 times its original volume. Those white, lightweight granules in your potting mix are the result of ancient volcanic eruptions processed through industrial kilns.

## What Is Perlite and Why Do Organic Growers Use It?

Perlite originates as volcanic obsidian containing 2-5% trapped water within its glassy structure. When heated rapidly in industrial kilns at 1,600°F, the bound water vaporizes and expands the glass into a cellular foam structure — creating thousands of microscopic air chambers in each granule. The resulting material weighs just 5-10 lbs per cubic foot compared to 75-100 lbs for garden soil.

Organic growers use perlite for one primary reason: it creates permanent, non-compressible drainage channels that prevent waterlogging and deliver oxygen to root zones. In heavy organic mixes rich in compost and worm castings, perlite prevents the dense biological material from becoming anaerobic.

## What Properties Does Perlite Bring to Soil?

Perlite's contribution is almost entirely physical rather than chemical. It has negligible nutrient content (no significant N-P-K value), near-zero cation exchange capacity, and a neutral pH of 6.5-7.5. Its value lies in its permanent porosity — each granule is roughly 93% void space by volume, creating air channels that persist even under compaction from watering and gravity.

The drainage capacity of perlite is measured at 30-50% by volume after saturation — meaning a mix containing 20% perlite retains significant air porosity even when thoroughly watered. This prevents root rot, promotes aerobic microbial activity, and reduces the risk of fungal pathogens like Pythium and Fusarium that thrive in waterlogged conditions.

## How Does Perlite Compare to Pumice?

Daley Organics uses both perlite and pumice in our soil blends, and the distinction matters. Perlite is lighter and creates larger air pockets, but it floats when watered heavily and can migrate to the soil surface over time. Pumice is denser, stays in place permanently, and has a measurable CEC (cation exchange capacity) that perlite lacks — meaning pumice holds and exchanges nutrients while perlite does not.

The complementary pairing works because perlite excels at immediate drainage in the upper root zone where oxygen demand is highest, while pumice provides stable structural porosity deeper in the profile where nutrient exchange matters more.

## How Does Daley Organics Use Perlite?

Perlite is a base media component in all three Daley Organics soil mixes: Daley's Mix ($140/yd), Premium Soil Mix ($130/yd), and Merlin Blend ($55/yd). It's blended with coco coir, peat moss, pumice, and premium compost to create the physical structure before the 25-ingredient fertilizer blend is added.

At our Grants Pass soil yard on Monument Drive, we use horticultural-grade perlite (#3 or #4 size — 1/8" to 3/8" diameter) rather than the fine-grade material used in seed starting. The coarser particle size creates larger drainage channels suited to the heavy organic load in our premium blends.

## How to Use Perlite in Your Garden

For container mixes, add perlite at 15-25% of total volume. For raised beds, 10-15% is sufficient since the deeper profile provides natural drainage. In seed-starting trays, fine-grade perlite at 30-40% ensures the delicate seedling roots never sit in waterlogged medium.

Perlite dust is a respiratory irritant — always moisten perlite before mixing, and wear a dust mask when handling large volumes. The dust settles quickly once wet and poses no ongoing concern in the finished soil mix.
